The problem

Grads know Excel in theory.
They freeze when asked to build.

India produces a flood of commerce, finance, and MBA graduates every year. Most have watched tutorials but never built a model under pressure — so they stumble in interviews and on day one of the job.

01

Passive learning

Courses are 90% video. You recognise VLOOKUP when you see it, but can't write it from a blank cell when it counts.

02

The college gap

Classrooms teach theory and accounting. Employers expect a working 3-statement model. Nobody bridges the two.

03

Interview reality

The "open Excel and build this" test filters out most candidates — not for lack of knowledge, but lack of reps.

The solution

Reps, not replays.

A live spreadsheet in the browser. Real company data, a real task, instant feedback on whether your formula is right. The way you actually get good at anything — by doing it, repeatedly.

Known constraint

The browser eats some shortcuts.
We've already planned for it.

Excel mastery is partly about keyboard speed — and browsers reserve some keys for themselves. We name this limitation up front, because how a product handles its own weak spot says more than its strengths.

⚠ The limitation

A few shortcuts are locked by the browser and OS for security and cannot be overridden by any web app — by design:

Ctrl+TCtrl+WCtrl+NCtrl+TabF5F11

These open tabs, close windows, and refresh — they aren't Excel skills anyway, but they do break perfect fidelity.

✓ How we handle it

When a cell is focused, the grid intercepts keystrokes and reproduces the shortcuts that actually matter for analyst work:

Ctrl+Shift+→F2F4Alt+=Ctrl+D

This is how Google Sheets and Excel-for-web already run. Free engines like Univer and Handsontable ship this keyboard layer — we inherit it, not rebuild it.

~88%
of analyst-relevant shortcuts reproduce in-browser. The unreachable ones are browser navigation, not modeling skills.
Reframe the promise

We grade modeling logic and formula correctness — the higher-value, hire-worthy skill — and treat raw shortcut speed as a transfer benefit, not the core claim. The constraint barely touches what we actually test.

Honesty as marketing

"Practice the logic here, your speed transfers to desktop Excel." Naming the limit up front pre-empts the complaint and builds trust with students and partners.

Risks & verdict

What could kill it — and the call.

  • RISK 01 · MONETISATIONFreemium conversion in India is low (often 1–3%). Without a compelling, outcome-tied paid tier, revenue starves the business.
  • RISK 02 · BUILD BURDENA reliable in-browser spreadsheet that grades formulas is real engineering. Mitigate by using existing free engines — don't rebuild what Quantus built.
  • RISK 03 · FREE ALTERNATIVESReal Excel, YouTube, and Microsoft Copilot are free. The product must clearly beat "just practice in Excel yourself."
